## If you read one thing, read this

This is a 1-credit seminar about what computer science actually *is*: the whole map of it, not just one corner. Over fifteen Wednesdays you'll meet artificial intelligence, security, systems, networks, data, open source, and the people who work in each. Then you'll pick a direction on purpose instead of by accident.

**About half of this class has never written a line of code.** That is expected, it is planned for, and it is not a disadvantage. Nothing here assumes you arrived knowing anything.

!!! tip "You are also in Intro to Programming"
    That course teaches you Java from the bottom up: variables, types, loops. This course works from the top down: what an algorithm is, how to break a problem apart, and how to use the tools professionals actually use. The two fit together on purpose. **This course does not teach Java.**

## Three things this course is trying to do

**Show you the whole map.** The middle of the semester goes to a different corner of the field each week, several of them with a guest who works in it. You cannot choose a direction you've never heard of.

**Build a cohort.** You'll be in a pod with a peer mentor, a student who was sitting where you are now. Attendance and participation are 20% of your grade because the room only works if you're in it.

**Teach you to work with AI, early and honestly.** You'll be directing a coding agent on your own code within the first month, for real work. The one rule is that you say what you used and what it got wrong. See the [AI Policy](ai-policy.md).

!!! info "Everything required in this course is free"
    Every tool is free for students, usually through the GitHub Student Developer Pack. No assignment will ever require a paid subscription. If cost is ever a barrier, email me. There is a loaner laptop path too.
